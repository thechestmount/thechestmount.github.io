Holbrook Police Burning Jeep Rescue: What OUI Second Offense Means in Massachusetts

Oct 26, 20267 min readBy The Chest Mount

Holbrook, MA officers pulled a trapped driver from a burning Jeep, and police are seeking an OUI second offense summons. What happened, what M.G.L. c. 90 §24 says about a second OUI, and how a summons works. General information, not legal advice.

Share Share on X Facebook Reddit

At 3:20 on a Sunday morning, a Jeep in Holbrook, Massachusetts, went off the road, snapped a utility pole in half and caught fire with the driver still inside. Bodycam footage provided by the Holbrook Police Department shows three officers getting him out in about a minute. Hours later, the department announced it is seeking a summons charging the driver with operating under the influence (OUI), second offense.

This explainer covers what police and news outlets have reported, what an OUI second offense means under Massachusetts law, and how the summons process works. It's general information, not legal advice. The driver hasn't been named, no criminal complaint has been issued, and every charge mentioned here is an allegation. He is presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court.

What happened on Pine Street, according to Holbrook police

The facts below come from the Holbrook Police Department's official release (Oct. 25, 2026), as also reported by WCVB, KHBS 40/29, South Coast Spotlight, CBS Boston and NBC Boston.

  • The crash. Around 3:20 a.m. on Sunday, Oct. 25, a Jeep left the roadway near 206 Pine St., struck a utility pole and a tree, and crashed into a retaining wall. Police say several nearby properties were damaged.
  • The scene. Officer Jonathan O'Brien arrived first and found the pole snapped in half, low-hanging wires and flames spreading across the front of the Jeep. Bystanders told him the driver was still inside.
  • The rescue. The driver had partly escaped, but his right foot was pinned beneath the damaged dashboard, and his foot and sneaker had caught fire. O'Brien worked to free the foot as Sgt. Martin Faherty and Officer Matthew Richards arrived. Police say the three officers pulled him free within about a minute of arriving, and Faherty and Richards moved him clear of the vehicle.
  • Afterward. Holbrook firefighters put out the fire, treated the driver and took him to South Shore Hospital in Weymouth with injuries believed to be non-life-threatening. O'Brien had minor burns to his fingers. WCVB reports this is the second time in the past year O'Brien has rescued a driver from a burning vehicle.
  • The charges sought. Police are seeking a summons for the driver, described only as a 37-year-old Holbrook man, for OUI of liquor (second offense), negligent operation of a motor vehicle, a marked lanes violation and speeding.

"These officers did an incredible job under dangerous conditions," Chief David Del Papa said. "Their quick response likely saved his life."

News outlets aired the bodycam, credited to Holbrook police. We don't host or embed it. Watch it through the linked reports.

What the bodycam shows, and what it doesn't

The footage captures the rescue: an officer running toward the fire, three officers working together, and the driver being moved to safety. What it doesn't show is anything that decides the OUI allegation, such as a blood alcohol level or what happened before the crash. Those questions are for the court. OUI vs. DUI: why Massachusetts says "OUI"

Massachusetts' drunk-driving law is M.G.L. chapter 90, section 24. It applies to anyone who operates a motor vehicle on a public way, or a place the public can access, either:

  • with a blood alcohol level of 0.08% or higher, or
  • while under the influence of intoxicating liquor, marijuana, narcotic drugs, depressants or stimulants.

Because the law is written around operating under the influence, the charge is called OUI. Other states say DUI or DWI.

What "second offense" means

"Second offense" means the state alleges the person had one prior "like" violation before the date of the new one. Under §24, a prior counts if the person was "previously convicted or assigned to an alcohol or controlled substance education, treatment, or rehabilitation program" by a Massachusetts court "or any other jurisdiction." So a prior case resolved through an alcohol education program, or one from another state, can count.

The statute doesn't set a look-back window. The Registry of Motor Vehicles says it considers a driver's "entire driving history," including out-of-state convictions, when counting prior OUIs.

The "second offense" part has to be proven, too. Under §24, a trial can't start or a plea be taken until the court gets the defendant's record from the probation commissioner, and certified records of a prior conviction are admissible "after the commonwealth has established the defendant's guilt on the primary offense." In other words, the new OUI is decided first, then the prior.

Penalties for a second OUI in Massachusetts

If a person is convicted of OUI with one qualifying prior, §24(1)(a)(1) says:

  • Fine: $600 to $10,000
  • Jail: 60 days to 2.5 years. The sentence can't be reduced below 30 days or suspended, and the person isn't eligible for probation, parole or furlough until 30 days are served.
  • Assessments: a $250 assessment (Head Injury Treatment Services Trust Fund) and a $50 assessment (Victims of Drunk Driving Trust Fund), neither of which can be waived

There is one alternative. Under §24(1)(a)(4), for someone with fewer than two priors, a judge may (with the defendant's consent and written findings) order two years of probation with at least 14 days in a residential alcohol treatment program plus outpatient counseling.

License consequences. A conviction revokes the license. For a second offense, the statute says it can't be restored until two years after conviction. A hardship license for work or school (up to 12 hours a day) can be requested after one year, and a broader limited license after 18 months. Under Melanie's Law, a hardship license for a second offense requires an ignition interlock device in every vehicle the person owns, leases or drives. The RMV says reinstatement requires passing a learner's permit exam and road test, and that the interlock stays for two years after full reinstatement.

Refusing a breath test. Separately, §24(f) says a driver with a prior OUI who refuses a chemical test after an OUI arrest faces an automatic 3-year license suspension.

Is a second OUI a felony in Massachusetts?

Under M.G.L. chapter 274, section 1, "a crime punishable by death or imprisonment in the state prison is a felony. All other crimes are misdemeanors." The second-offense penalty in §24 tops out at 2.5 years and doesn't include a state prison option, so it falls on the misdemeanor side. A third or later offense can bring state prison time.

The other charges: negligent operation, marked lanes and speeding

Negligent operation is also in §24, at subsection (2)(a). It covers operating "negligently so that the lives or safety of the public might be endangered." The penalty is a fine of $20 to $200, or 2 weeks to 2 years in jail, or both, plus a $250 assessment. The marked lanes and speeding counts are traffic violations.

What "seeking a summons" means

In Massachusetts, when someone isn't arrested, police usually apply to the district court for a criminal complaint. Under M.G.L. chapter 218, section 35A, the person generally gets a chance to be heard, personally or through a lawyer, before a complaint for a misdemeanor issues. The court issues process only if there's probable cause. Until then, there's no formal charge, which is why Holbrook police say they can't release the driver's name.

A complaint is still only an accusation. Our detained vs. arrested explainer and Know Your Rights page cover the basics of what happens after a police encounter.

How to request the records in Massachusetts

Bodycam video, the crash report and the incident report are generally requested under the Massachusetts Public Records Law, though records in an open investigation can be withheld or redacted. Send a written request to the department's records access officer with the date, time and location (here, Oct. 25, 2026, around 3:20 a.m., near 206 Pine St.). Frequently asked questions

What happened in the Holbrook burning Jeep rescue?

According to the Holbrook Police Department, around 3:20 a.m. on Sunday, Oct. 25, 2026, a Jeep left the road near 206 Pine St., hit a utility pole and a tree, crashed into a retaining wall and caught fire. The driver's right foot was pinned under the dashboard. Sgt. Martin Faherty and Officers Jonathan O'Brien and Matthew Richards freed him within about a minute of arriving and pulled him clear. He was taken to South Shore Hospital with injuries believed to be non-life-threatening, and Officer O'Brien had minor burns to his fingers.

Has the Holbrook driver been charged?

Not yet, as of Oct. 26, 2026. Holbrook police say they are seeking a summons on charges of OUI (second offense), negligent operation, a marked lanes violation and speeding. The court has not issued a criminal complaint, so the driver hasn't been named. These are allegations, and he is presumed innocent.

What is the punishment for a second OUI in Massachusetts?

Under M.G.L. c. 90, §24(1)(a)(1), a person convicted of OUI who has one prior like conviction or program assignment faces a fine of $600 to $10,000 and 60 days to 2.5 years in jail, with at least 30 days that can't be suspended or reduced. The statute also lets a judge, in some cases, order two years of probation with at least 14 days in a residential alcohol treatment program instead. The RMV lists a two-year license loss and a required ignition interlock device. This is general information, not legal advice.

Is OUI a felony in Massachusetts?

Massachusetts law (M.G.L. c. 274, §1) defines a felony as a crime punishable by death or imprisonment in state prison. The second-offense penalty in chapter 90, §24 is up to 2.5 years and doesn't include a state prison option, so it falls on the misdemeanor side of that definition. A third or later offense can be punished by state prison time.

What is the difference between OUI and DUI?

They describe the same kind of offense. Massachusetts law is written around operating a motor vehicle while under the influence, so the charge is called OUI. Many other states use DUI or DWI.
