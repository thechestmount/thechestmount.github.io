Timberlake Police Cruiser Hit During Traffic Stop: What OVI and Ohio's Move Over Law Mean

Oct 28, 20266 min readBy The Chest Mount

A Timberlake, OH officer had stepped out of his cruiser about 20 seconds before a suspected impaired driver hit it. What police and news reports say, what OVI means under ORC 4511.19 and what Ohio's move over law (ORC 4511.213) requires. General information, not legal advice.

Share Share on X Facebook Reddit

It was a routine traffic stop just before 1 a.m. on a Sunday. Officer Trevor Harris of the Timberlake, Ohio, Police Department pulled a car over on Lakeshore Boulevard and stepped out of his cruiser. Body camera video posted by the department shows what happened about 20 seconds later: the sound of impact as another vehicle slammed into the back of his patrol car.

The cruiser was totaled. Harris walked away uninjured. The driver accused of hitting it is now charged with OVI, Ohio's term for impaired driving.

This explainer covers what police and news outlets have reported, what OVI means under Ohio law, and what Ohio's move over law asks of every driver who comes up on a stopped police car. It's general information, not legal advice. The driver has pleaded not guilty, and every charge mentioned here is an allegation. He is presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court.

What happened on Lakeshore Boulevard, according to police and news reports

The facts below come from WKYC, Cleveland 19 (Oct. 25 and Oct. 27), WHIO, News 5 Cleveland and FOX 8, which cite the Timberlake and Eastlake police departments.

  • The stop. Around 12:48 to 12:50 a.m. on Sunday, Oct. 25, 2026, Officer Harris was conducting a traffic stop on Lakeshore Boulevard near Carlton Drive in Lake County.
  • The crash. WHIO reports the department's bodycam shows the cruiser was hit about 20 seconds after Harris got out. FOX 8, citing Eastlake police, reports the cruiser was hit from behind.
  • The damage. Cleveland 19 reports the cruiser was totaled. Harris was not injured, and police say the driver of the vehicle that hit it was not injured either.
  • The stopped car. Reports differ here. WKYC says the driver slammed into the cruiser "and another vehicle," and a voice off camera can be heard saying, "He just hit my car." FOX 8 reports Eastlake police said the stopped vehicle was not hit and its driver wasn't hurt.
  • The arrest. Eastlake officers nearby heard the crash and responded. Eastlake police arrested the driver, a 40-year-old Eastlake man, for OVI and several other traffic offenses. Timberlake police say there were "multiple full and empty alcoholic beverages" in his vehicle.
  • In court. Cleveland 19 reports he pleaded not guilty in Willoughby Municipal Court on Tuesday, Oct. 27, without appearing in person. The judge set a pretrial for Nov. 3 and ordered him not to drive or drink alcohol.

A driver at the scene told Cleveland 19 she was handing the officer her license when "he ran into the woods." "So, I white-knuckled my steering wheel hoping that I wasn't going to get hit. All of a sudden, heard a big crash and a lot of noise."

"One of the most gut wrenching calls to receive as a Chief of Police is that a squad is en route to your Officer," the department wrote on Facebook. Cleveland 19 adds that Harris was recognized at a Mothers Against Drunk Driving (MADD) banquet in Columbus this month for 25 DUI arrests in 2025.

The Timberlake Police Department posted the bodycam on its Facebook page, and the outlets above aired it. We don't host or embed it. Watch it through the linked reports.

What the bodycam shows, and what it doesn't

The footage captures how fast the moment unfolds: an officer out of his car, the sound of the impact, and the scramble right after. What it doesn't show is anything that decides the OVI charge, such as a test result or what happened inside the other vehicle beforehand. That's for the court. What does OVI mean in Ohio?

OVI stands for operating a vehicle under the influence. Other states say DUI, DWI or, in Massachusetts, OUI (see our Holbrook explainer). Ohio's law is Ohio Revised Code 4511.19, and it gives prosecutors two main routes:

  • Impairment. Operating a vehicle while "under the influence of alcohol, a drug of abuse, or a combination of them" (division (A)(1)(a)). This doesn't require a specific number.
  • Prohibited concentration ("per se"). Operating with a test result at or above set limits, for example 0.08% alcohol in whole blood or 0.08 grams per 210 liters of breath. The statute also sets limits for blood serum, urine and listed drugs.

At 0.17% or higher, the "high tier" divisions apply and the mandatory penalties go up. Drivers under 21 face a separate, lower limit starting at 0.02%.

The news reports don't say which division of the law applies here, what any test showed, or what the "other traffic offenses" are. We won't guess.

OVI penalties in Ohio: a first offense

For a first OVI (no prior within ten years), ORC 4511.19(G)(1)(a) says it's a first-degree misdemeanor, and the court must impose:

  • Jail: a mandatory three consecutive days. The court can suspend it and instead order a three-day certified drivers' intervention program. Total jail can't exceed six months.
  • Fine: $565 to $1,075.
  • License: a suspension of one to three years. The court may grant limited driving privileges, or unlimited privileges with an ignition interlock device.

A high-tier first offense requires at least three days in jail plus the three-day program or, in some cases, at least six days in jail. The penalties climb with priors: a second offense within ten years carries a 10-day mandatory jail term (20 days at the high tier) and a one-to-seven-year suspension. Three or four priors within ten years can make it a fourth-degree felony.

None of this applies unless there's a conviction. The case is at the pretrial stage, and the driver has pleaded not guilty.

Ohio's move over law: what ORC 4511.213 requires

This case is also a reminder of a law many drivers half-remember. ORC 4511.213 applies when you approach a stationary public safety vehicle, emergency vehicle, road service vehicle, waste collection vehicle, highway maintenance vehicle or certain PUCO inspection vehicles displaying flashing, oscillating or rotating lights. You must:

  1. On a road with at least two lanes going your direction: proceed with due caution and, if possible and safe given road, weather and traffic conditions, change into a lane that isn't next to the stopped vehicle.
  2. On any other road, or if changing lanes isn't possible or safe: proceed with due caution, reduce speed and keep a safe speed for conditions.

Penalties. A violation is a minor misdemeanor. It becomes a fourth-degree misdemeanor with one prior traffic conviction within a year, and a third-degree misdemeanor with two or more. The court must impose double the usual fine, and an extra fine can apply if distracted driving contributed. The statute calls it a strict liability offense, meaning the state doesn't have to prove intent.

None of the reports say the driver here was charged under this section. We're explaining the law because the crash happened at a traffic stop, not suggesting which charges apply.

How to request the records in Ohio

Bodycam video, the crash report and the incident report are generally requested under Ohio's Public Records Act, ORC 149.43. Records tied to an open case can be withheld or redacted, and the law lets agencies charge the actual cost of preparing video, capped at $75 per hour of video and $750 total. Send a written request to the Timberlake (or Eastlake) police records custodian with the date, time and location (Oct. 25, 2026, around 12:48 a.m., Lakeshore Blvd. near Carlton Dr.). Frequently asked questions

What happened to the Timberlake police cruiser?

According to the Timberlake Police Department and news reports, around 12:48 to 12:50 a.m. on Sunday, Oct. 25, 2026, Officer Trevor Harris had stopped a car on Lakeshore Boulevard near Carlton Drive and gotten out of his cruiser. WHIO reports the department's body camera video shows the cruiser was hit about 20 seconds after he got out. The cruiser was totaled, and Officer Harris was not injured.

Was the driver charged?

Yes. Eastlake police arrested a 40-year-old Eastlake man, who is charged with OVI and several other traffic-related offenses, according to Cleveland 19. He pleaded not guilty in Willoughby Municipal Court on Oct. 27, 2026, and a pretrial is set for Nov. 3. These are allegations, and he is presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court.

What does OVI mean in Ohio?

OVI stands for operating a vehicle under the influence. Under Ohio Revised Code 4511.19, it covers operating a vehicle while under the influence of alcohol, a drug of abuse or both, or with a prohibited concentration, such as 0.08% or more alcohol in whole blood or 0.08 grams or more per 210 liters of breath.

What is the penalty for a first OVI in Ohio?

Under ORC 4511.19(G)(1)(a), a first offense is generally a first-degree misdemeanor with a mandatory three-day jail term (or, in some cases, a three-day drivers' intervention program), a fine of $565 to $1,075 and a license suspension of one to three years. Higher test results, prior offenses and other factors change the penalties. This is general information, not legal advice.

What does Ohio's move over law require?

Under ORC 4511.213, drivers approaching a stationary public safety vehicle, emergency vehicle and certain other vehicles with flashing lights must move to a non-adjacent lane on a multi-lane road if it's possible and safe. Otherwise they must slow down and proceed with due caution. A violation starts as a minor misdemeanor, and the court must double the usual fine.
