When Refusing to Leave Goes Wrong: Marion, IL Bodycam Breakdown

Oct 20, 20266 min readBy The Chest Mount

A neutral, minute-by-minute breakdown of the Marion, Illinois police bodycam footage from March 26, 2026: a trespass warning, a refusal to leave, taser use and what officers say afterward.

Share Share on X Facebook Reddit

The most-watched full video on The Chest Mount so far is a slow-building encounter from Marion, Illinois. It starts as a conversation about whether a man has to leave a building and ends with a taser deployment, a struggle on the floor and officers searching for a missing taser. Below is a neutral walkthrough of what the full police bodycam footage shows, built only from the video itself and its captions.

Everything here is described the way it appears on camera. Where we quote someone, it comes from the audio. The man in the video is presumed innocent, and nothing in this post should be read as a finding of guilt.

The setup: a call about a disorderly man

The video opens with an on-screen caption: "On March 26, 2026, officers in Marion, Illinois, respond to a man threatening public officials and being disorderly." That caption is the channel's summary of the call. It is not a court finding, and the footage that follows is the only primary record we have.

When the first officer walks in, the man is seated and talking. His first words set up the entire dispute: he says he was asked to leave, "but I'm sitting here talking. Why would I leave if I'm sitting here talking?" He tells the officer he is not banned from the place and that nobody can ban him "without anything I've done wrong."

The trespass warning

About a minute in, the officer explains why he is there. According to the footage, he tells the man that the facility wants him "trespassed from the premises because you were causing issues." The man answers, "I did not."

What follows is a textbook example of how a trespass warning is delivered in real time. The man argues that "you can't be trespassed from a place until you're told you're not allowed to be there." The officer replies, "I'm telling you you're not allowed to be there... Now you're officially trespassed from the premises."

The man pushes back that the officer "can't trespass me from a public state facility." Someone in the room says the building is not government-funded. This disagreement about whether the space is public or private is never resolved on camera, and it is a good reminder that trespass rules depend on who controls the property and on state law.

Repeated requests to leave

For roughly two minutes, the officer asks the man to gather his things and go. The exchange is calm on the officer's side and repetitive:

  • "I'm going to need you to get your stuff, and you're going to have to head out."
  • "Look, man, just head out the door, and we can be peaceful. Okay?"
  • "There's no reason to go to jail or get in trouble, man. All I'm doing is asking you to leave."

The man repeatedly says he is not going anywhere. When the officer says he will make him leave if he has to, the man answers, "You can't put your hands on me, you know that." At one point the officer asks whether the man is on pre-trial conditions. The footage does not answer that question.

At about the 2:30 mark the officer gives what he calls "one more chance" and asks him to "grab your stuff and head out of here and take the trespass." The man says, "I'm not taking no trespass." The officer then warns that if he comes back to the property "it's just going to be a bigger charge."

The moment it turns physical

Just after the three-minute mark the officer says, "This is your last warning." The man answers, "Don't you dare put your hands on me." Seconds later the officer takes hold of him and the commands change to "Grab your stuff" and "Give me your hands," repeated many times while the man repeats "Get your hands off me."

At one point the man says, "I'm going to leave," and the officer answers, "No, you had a chance to leave." That exchange is one of the moments viewers debate most in the comments, because it shows the point where the situation moved from a request to leave into an arrest.

Taser use, according to the footage

Around 4:37 an officer shouts "Taser, taser, taser." What follows on the floor is chaotic: officers repeatedly order "Turn around," "Put your hands behind your back" and "Roll over," and the man repeatedly refuses or says he is not trying to fight.

Several moments are worth noting, all as they are heard in the audio:

  • An officer says, "I already tased him twice."
  • Someone says, "No, I lost my taser."
  • After the man is restrained, an officer says, "Where's his taser? ... We need to find his taser."
  • An officer later says the man "has my handcuffs, taser, and phone."

Afterward, the officer gives his own account on camera: "I tased him. I drive stunned him twice. Then I tased him. He grabbed my taser. Pulled out of my hand." If you are not sure what that distinction means, our explainer on what drive-stun means on a taser covers the difference between drive-stun and probe deployment. Later, someone confirms "both barbs are accounted for."

This part of the encounter is the focus of the Shorts series, including He Thought He Could Stay, Then This Happened and The Moment He Realized He Made a Huge Mistake.

What the officer says afterward

The final section of the video is quieter and, for many viewers, the most revealing. The officer explains to others what happened, in his own words: he says he told the man to leave "a few times," that the man said "or what?", and that he answered, "I'm going to have to make you leave and I don't want to."

He also says, "I had told him to leave twice in the last few weeks. I never officially trespassed [him] in here," which explains why the formal trespass warning happened on this day.

Later, speaking to someone at the scene, he says: "I did not want it to go down like that. I know who he is. I've dealt with him before... I was really just hoping he was just get up and leave." Near the end, an officer mentions that one of the charges will be "disarming a peace officer," and an officer says he needs to wash out a bite mark. The video does not show charging documents or any court outcome.

What this footage can and cannot tell you

Bodycam video is powerful, but it has limits. If you want to judge this encounter fairly, keep a few things in mind (we cover these in depth in how to watch bodycam footage critically):

  • The camera shows one point of view. It is mounted on an officer's chest, so you see what he faces, not the whole room.
  • We don't see what happened before. The officer references earlier warnings and the opening caption mentions threats, but none of that is on this video.
  • Audio is incomplete. Auto-captions miss words, and several statements overlap.
  • Charges are not convictions. Mentions of possible charges are just that. The man is presumed innocent.

Frequently asked questions

Where and when did this encounter happen?

According to the opening caption of the video, it happened on March 26, 2026, in Marion, Illinois.

Why was the man asked to leave?

In the footage, the officer says the people running the facility want him trespassed from the premises because he was "causing issues." The man disputes this on camera and says he did nothing wrong.

Was a taser used?

Yes, according to the footage. After the encounter an officer says on camera that he drive-stunned the man twice and then deployed the taser, and that the man pulled the taser out of his hand.

Was the man convicted of anything?

The footage does not show any court outcome. Everyone shown is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty in a court of law.
